# Does the tRNA pool predict how yeast actually translates?

**The question:** `scripts/elongation.py` computes which codons the yeast tRNA
pool serves well, from **tRNA gene copy numbers alone**. Gate 4 of
[`protocol/layer-architecture-v1.md`](../protocol/layer-architecture-v1.md)
requires a layer to recover something it was not shown. The layer had only
passed the weak form: it reproduced the known arginine codon ordering, which
shows the wiring is right and nothing more.

**This is the real test, against two external measurements neither chosen nor
generated by this project.**

Run: `python -I scripts/validate_elongation.py`

---

## Verdict first

| Test | Result | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **Per-codon decoding speed** | **rho = −0.62**, p = 2e-07, 58 codons | The layer predicts which codons are slow |
| **Beats codon counting?** | **yes**, −0.62 vs −0.54 | The tRNA data earns its place |
| Per-gene translation efficiency | rho = +0.44, p < 1e-220, 4,820 genes | Holds, but partly circular |
| Beats amino acid composition? | **yes**, +0.44 vs +0.26 | Codon choice carries real signal |
| Beats a shuffled-weight null? | **yes**, 0 of 200 permutations reached it | The specific tRNA link matters |
| **Magnitudes** | **27,000× predicted vs 2.7× measured** | **The ordering is right; the scale is not** |

**The layer passes gate 4. It also fails in a specific, useful way, and that
failure is the most important line in this report.**

---

## Test 1 — per-codon decoding speed, the non-circular test

**The measurement.** Gardin J, Yeasmin R, Yurovsky A, Cai Y, Skiena S, Futcher B
(2014) *Measurement of average decoding rates of the 61 sense codons in vivo*.
eLife 3:e03735, Table 2A: **ribosome residence time** at the A site for each
sense codon. Higher residence time means the ribosome lingers, so the codon is
decoded more slowly. Retrieved from the PubMed Central full-text XML and parsed
programmatically into
[`data/gardin/rrt_table2.tsv`](../data/gardin/rrt_table2.tsv).

**The prediction, fixed before looking.** Residence time is a *positional*
measurement of speed, not an expression level, so the circularity that weakens
test 2 does not apply. A codon the tRNA pool serves well should be passed
through quickly, so **adaptiveness and residence time must be negatively
correlated.**

> **rho = −0.623, p = 1.8e-07, across 58 codons.**

The extremes line up:

| Slowest measured | residence | our weight | | Fastest measured | residence | our weight |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|---:|---:|
| CTC Leu | 1.89 | **0.062** | | ACC Thr | 0.70 | 0.488 |
| CCC Pro | 1.71 | **0.089** | | AAG Lys | 0.74 | **1.000** |
| GGG Gly | 1.61 | 0.182 | | GTT Val | 0.75 | **0.862** |
| AGG Arg | 1.59 | 0.278 | | GTC Val | 0.75 | 0.621 |
| ATA Ile | 1.57 | **0.123** | | GAT Asp | 0.76 | 0.581 |

**The two slowest codons in the cell are the two our model marks nearly
unserved, and it reached that from gene counts in a genome annotation.**

### The control that matters: does it beat just counting codons?

Codon usage frequency is free. It needs no tRNA data and it is printed in the
same source table.

| Predictor | rho vs residence time |
|---|---:|
| **Our tRNA-derived weights** | **−0.623** |
| Codon usage frequency | −0.536 |

**The tRNA pool predicts decoding speed better than codon usage does.** That is
the result that justifies the layer existing rather than a lookup table of
codon frequencies.

## Test 2 — per-gene translation efficiency

**The measurement.** Weinberg DE, Shah P, Eichhorn SW, Hussmann JA, Plotkin JB,
Bartel DP (2016) *Improved ribosome-footprint and mRNA measurements provide
insights into dynamics and regulation of yeast translation*. Cell Rep
14:1787-1799. GEO **GSE75897**: ribosome-footprint density and mRNA abundance
per gene. Coding sequences from SGD S288C.

**5,077 genes scored** out of 6,039 coding sequences, after intersecting with
both measurement tables.

| mRNA cutoff | genes | our weights | composition only |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| none | 5,077 | **+0.429** | +0.260 |
| ≥ 1 RPKM | 4,994 | **+0.435** | +0.261 |
| ≥ 5 RPKM | 4,820 | **+0.439** | +0.262 |
| ≥ 10 RPKM | 4,658 | **+0.448** | +0.265 |
| ≥ 50 RPKM | 2,412 | **+0.437** | +0.249 |

Cutoffs are **swept rather than chosen**, for the same reason
`growth_coupling.py` sweeps module cost: a single cutoff would be a number with
no source.

### The circularity, stated before the result and not after

**This test is weaker than it looks.** The tAI constants were optimised to
correlate with codon usage bias, and codon usage bias is strongest in highly
expressed genes. A positive correlation with translation efficiency is partly
built in. **The controls are what carry the weight.**

**Control 1 — codon choice vs amino acid composition.** Replace every codon's
weight with the average for its amino acid, keeping *what the protein is made
of* and discarding *which synonymous codon was picked*.

**+0.44 with codon choice, +0.26 without. A gap of +0.18 at every cutoff.** The
tRNA pool is not a proxy for residue composition.

**Control 2 — permutation null.** Shuffle the 60 weights among the 60 codons,
breaking the link to the tRNA genes, 200 times.

| | |
|---|---:|
| observed | **+0.439** |
| permuted median | 0.127 |
| permuted 95th percentile | 0.345 |
| permuted maximum | 0.400 |
| permutations reaching the observed value | **0 of 200** |

Empirical p < 0.005. **Note the tail: the best shuffle reached 0.400 against an
observed 0.439.** The margin is real and it is not large, because shuffling
preserves the distribution of weights and genes still differ in composition.

### The confound, measured rather than hidden

| | tAI vs footprint density | tAI vs mRNA abundance |
|---|---:|---:|
| all genes | +0.613 | +0.605 |
| ≥ 5 RPKM | +0.647 | **+0.639** |

**Our weights correlate with mRNA abundance almost exactly as strongly as with
ribosome footprints** — and the layer makes no claim whatsoever about mRNA
abundance. Codon adaptiveness is largely a marker of *how highly expressed a
gene is*, which is a property of evolution, not of translation speed. Dividing
footprints by mRNA removes most of that, and +0.44 is what survives. **That
number, not the +0.65, is the honest one.**

## The failure worth more than the pass

**The layer ranks codons well and gets their magnitudes badly wrong.**

| | best-to-worst span |
|---|---:|
| Our predicted weights | **about 27,000×** |
| Measured residence times | **2.7×** |

**CGA is the clearest case.** The model calls it effectively unreadable, weight
near zero, because yeast has no tRNA gene whose anticodon reads it
Watson-Crick. The measurement puts it slow but unremarkable, residence time
1.45 against a maximum of 1.89. **Wobble decoding still works. It just works
poorly, and gene copy number alone cannot see the difference between "poorly"
and "not at all."**

This is the quantitative argument for Gate 3 refusing to print a rate. A model
whose dynamic range is four orders of magnitude out would have produced
confident, badly wrong numbers in codons per second.

---

## A defect in the source, not silently patched

The published Gardin Table 2A **lists `TCC` twice**, with different usage and
residence-time values, and lists no `TCT`. Serine is one codon short.

The usage figures make the likely explanation obvious. **Both rows were excluded
anyway**, which is why 58 codons were used rather than 60. Editing another
group's published data on our own inference is not something this project does,
and the loader raises the duplicate rather than quietly keeping the last row.

## What is not done, and what this does not establish

- **Nothing is tested about heterologous protein.** Every gene here is a native
  yeast gene with millions of years of adaptation to this exact tRNA pool. **A
  foreign gene has none of that**, and the chassis exists to express foreign
  genes. This is the largest gap in the whole validation.
- **One condition.** Both datasets are unstressed cells in rich medium. The
  chassis targets a cell forced to overproduce a foreign protein, where the tRNA
  pool itself may be depleted.
- **Footprint density is not elongation speed.** High occupancy can mean fast
  initiation or slow elongation; the ratio used here cannot separate them.
- **Still no rate.** Nothing here calibrates adaptiveness to codons per second,
  and after the dynamic-range result, nothing should.
- **Ribosome profiling is method-sensitive.** How cells are harvested affects
  the profile; this project did not evaluate that and took both datasets as
  published.
- **The chassis object has not been rewired.** Capability A1 in
  `chassis/chassis-v1.json` still reads *"native, confidence high"* with the
  note *"yeast codon tables well characterised"*. The layer now has a measured
  correlation behind it and the chassis still does not consume it.
- **No tRNA modification.** Wobble decoding is summarised by one constant per
  pairing type, which the CGA result suggests is the specific thing limiting
  accuracy.
- **Layer 2 still fails gate 4.** Growth coupling corrected an error inside this
  project; it has not recovered an external measurement. Of eight layers, **one
  now passes all five gates on real evidence.**
