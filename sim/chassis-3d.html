What the slider does

Growth falls as foreign protein takes over the cell. What you harvest is how much each cell makes times how fast cells multiply, so the best point is half the ceiling.

What this cannot tell you

  • The geometry is schematic. Compartment shapes and positions are drawn to be read, not to be structurally accurate. The topology of the route — nucleus, ER, Golgi, vesicle, out — is the part that is correct.
  • No titre, no yield, no probability of success. The staples and glycan trees you watch being attached are counts read off the sequence, not a prediction that the cell will attach them successfully.
  • Codon adaptiveness ranks codons and cannot give a rate. It is computed from 275 yeast tRNA genes and agrees with measured per-codon decoding speed at ρ = −0.62. But its predicted spread between best and worst codon is about 27,000× where the measured spread is 2.7×, so nothing in this page moves faster or slower because of it.
  • Only 1 of 22 capabilities has quantitative physics behind it. That is A1. The other 21 are categorical judgements, and six of the thresholds driving them have no published source at all — including the round 1000-residue number that decides whether a module gets built.
  • The division rate is first-order physics. Growth is taken to fall linearly with foreign protein and reach zero at 40%. That shape is well supported in E. coli and much less tested in yeast, and the source says “exceeding 40%”, so the optimum shown is a lower bound.
  • No module cost has been measured. Where the slider sits is a what-if. Nobody knows what any of these five modules actually costs the cell, so the load cannot be derived from the design.
  • This chassis does not exist. It is baker's yeast plus five proposed modules, each carrying published precedent rather than a validated construct. The cell you are orbiting is a specification.
  • The signal peptide and transmembrane calls are hydropathy heuristics, marked in the readout. Real answers need SignalP and DeepTMHMM.
